## Chapter 1: Doing the work, and the ceiling it hits

**"a Commodore VIC-20"**: Commodore's first colour home computer. It came out in Japan in 1980 as the VIC-1001 and reached Europe and the US in 1981.
<!-- checked 22 Nov 2026: computinghistory.org.uk, "Commodore releases the VIC-20 in Europe and US" (May 1981); Commodore VIC-20 collection entry -->

**"They bought me a Commodore 64"**: the VIC-20's successor, with 64 KB of memory and much better sound and graphics. It went on sale in August 1982.
<!-- checked 22 Nov 2026: Wikipedia VIC-20 (C64 unveiled summer 1982); Retro Gamer, "40 Years of the Commodore 64" (on sale August 1982) -->

**"Input, a magazine that printed listings"**: a weekly part-work from Marshall Cavendish, published in 52 issues across 1984 and 1985 and meant to be collected into binders.
<!-- checked 22 Nov 2026: Input Magazine (Wikipedia mirror); RetroKingSimon blog, "INPUT Magazine: A Retrospective" -->

**"comparative advantage"**: David Ricardo, *On the Principles of Political Economy and Taxation* (1817).
<!-- standard reference; title and year to confirm against a library record at polish -->

**"a Microsoft language service called LUIS"**: Language Understanding (LUIS). Microsoft stopped new LUIS resources from April 2023, closed its portal on 31 October 2025, and retired the service fully on 31 March 2026, pointing users to its successor, conversational language understanding.
